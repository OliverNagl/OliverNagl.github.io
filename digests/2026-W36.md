### 1. [read] Creating bottom-up RNA transfer vehicles from synthetic protein assemblies.
Schuhmacher MK, …, Wurst W · Nature · 2 Sep · `assembly`

> The authors fuse AI-generated protein-assembly shells of novel, non-viral sizes and shapes with natural RNA-packaging domains to build over 100 synthetic RNA transfer vehicles, then retarget them by grafting de novo peptide binders onto the shell surface. Unlike viral capsids, which converged evolutionarily onto a narrow range of sizes and shapes, or lipid nanoparticles, which have no genetically encoded, modularly retargetable shell, these designed assemblies access geometries outside that evolved design space and deliver RNA several orders of magnitude more efficiently, with tropism swapped out via appended binders rather than relying on a natural capsid protein. It is direct evidence that novel generative-design assembly architectures are not just structurally stable curiosities but a viable, functionalizable design space for real delivery tasks, validated all the way to in vivo biodistribution and a pig gene-editing model.

### 2. [read] Design of peptides with non-canonical amino acids using flow matching
Jin Sub Lee, Philip M. Kim · Bioinformatics · 31 Aug · `design-method` · code ✔

> NCFlow is a flow matching model that places an arbitrary non-canonical amino acid into a given backbone position using only its atom types and bond connectivity, having pretrained on millions of generic small-molecule structures and protein-ligand complexes before finetuning on the sparse set of ncAAs actually observed in PDB structures. Existing structure-based tools either can't model ncAAs at all or are locked to the small fixed vocabulary they were trained on, because in-protein ncAA data is scarce and biased; by learning atom- and bond-level chemistry instead of memorizing a closed vocabulary, NCFlow generalizes to ncAAs never seen during training and beats AlphaFold3-based methods on their structure prediction. For a designer this is a concrete, generalizable route to place arbitrary non-canonical side chains into a fixed backbone and score the result with a combined deep-learning-plus-alchemical-MD pipeline, useful for extending any backbone-design workflow to include ncAA side chains rather than being limited to the handful of ncAAs any given structure predictor has actually seen.

[url](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag654) · [doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag654) · [pdf](https://academic.oup.com/bioinformatics/advance-article-pdf/doi/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag654/70885944/btag654.pdf) · [code](https://github.com/mjslee0921/ncflow)

### 3. [read] Programmable design of synthetic plant immune receptors for pathogen protein recognition.
Zhu H, …, Gao C · Science (New York, N.Y.) · 3 Sep · `function-design`

> De novo-designed binder domains targeting specific pathogen proteins are grafted into the integrated decoy domain of a natural plant immune receptor scaffold, and the resulting chimeric receptors are then improved with a round of in-planta directed evolution that boosts immune activation while suppressing spontaneous autoactivation. Natural plant immune receptors have a pathogen-recognition repertoire fixed by evolution and too narrow for fast-evolving pathogens; here specificity becomes fully programmable by swapping in any de novo binder module, and the directed-evolution step closes the gap between a binder that merely binds its target and one that correctly triggers the receptor's native activation mechanism without firing on its own — a gap pure computational design didn't close alone. The transferable pattern is graft-a-designed-binder-into-a-natural-sensor-scaffold-then-evolve-for-activation-fidelity, directly applicable to any project chimerizing a designed binder onto natural signaling or entry machinery, and a reminder to budget an experimental evolution or screening round rather than trust computational binder scoring to guarantee correct downstream triggering.

[url](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42490461/) · [doi](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aee1792)

### 4. [read] Lenacapavir allosterically remodels the HIV-1 capsid.
Dos Santos NFB, …, Pornillos O · Science advances · 2 Sep · `assembly` · watchlist: Sundquist WI

> Lenacapavir binds the HIV-1 capsid lattice and allosterically alters the noncovalent bonding between subunits, and the authors show this ruptures the fullerene-cone lattice in two mechanical steps: first at the high-curvature pentameric declinations, then as fissures that propagate through the hexameric body, formalized as a stress-strain model linking local curvature change to global lattice failure. This overturns the simpler picture of lenacapavir as a lattice-hyperstabilizer that blocks uncoating; instead its potency comes from destabilizing specific high-curvature points first and letting failure cascade from there, a defined two-step mechanical sequence rather than uniform stabilization or single-step collapse. For anyone designing or perturbing curved icosahedral or fullerene-like lattices, this pinpoints the pentameric declinations as the mechanically weakest points where a small interface perturbation is disproportionately amplified into large-scale disassembly, which is a concrete failure mode to check for around any designed lattice's symmetry-breaking vertices.

### 6. [read] Toward De Novo Protein Design from Natural Language
Dai, F., …, Yuan, F. · bioRxiv · 31 Aug · `design-method`

> Pinal is a 16-billion-parameter foundation model trained on 1.7 billion synthetic protein-text pairs that translates a free-text functional description directly into a de novo protein sequence and structure, grounding the language in biophysical structural principles rather than starting from a template. Current design paradigms instead require either an existing protein to evolve or scaffold from, or deep family-specific expertise to hand-build the design strategy; Pinal skips both, and was validated across four distinct functional classes (a fluorescent protein, two enzymes, a metabolic protein), with the enzyme designs achieving catalytic turnover and one outperforming its natural counterpart. For a designer this establishes natural language as a plausible top-level instruction interface upstream of structure generation, though the demonstration here is entirely on monomeric functional proteins — whether text can steer generation toward a target symmetric assembly rather than a single fold is untested and would need to be checked before relying on it for multimeric design.

### 7. [read] Hierarchical discrete representations for coarse-to-fine protein conformation generation.
On S, …, Kim ES · Bioinformatics (Oxford, England) · 31 Aug · `struct-pred` · code ✔

> The model discretizes protein structure into hierarchical VQ-VAE codebooks, first generating a coarse code that fixes global topology and secondary structure, then conditioning on that coarse scaffold to progressively fill in fine-grained local geometry. Continuous-coordinate diffusion models like ESMDiff have no clean way to impose hard structural priors, so they trade off conformational diversity against structural validity; separating a coarse, prior-constrained code from a locally refined one lets this model keep both, and it beats ESMDiff on apo/holo and fold-switching benchmarks. ### [track] Anomalous temperature dependence in phase transitions via ballistic coalescence
Nalina Vadakkayil, …, Subir K. Das · arXiv · 31 Aug · `ml-method`

> A ballistic-aggregation theory for phase-separating Lennard-Jones clusters shows the cluster-growth exponent depends non-monotonically on temperature and can switch from the classical algebraic growth law to an exponentially fast 'explosive' regime once clusters move ballistically rather than diffusively.

[url](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30918v1) · [pdf](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30918v1)

Connection: Oracles that score whether a designed subunit will assemble typically evaluate static thermodynamic compatibility (interface energy, geometric fit) and are blind to whether the growth process itself falls into a different kinetic class before reaching the target closed shell. This paper's mechanism -- that the growth-law class (algebraic vs. explosive) is set by the mobility/collision regime of the growing clusters, not by the final free-energy minimum -- suggests an assembly oracle should also score the kinetic regime a subunit's collision dynamics would fall into, since a mechanistically sound design can still be kinetically trapped or run away into off-target aggregates depending on which growth-law regime its diffusivity and concentration place it in.

Confidence: medium
