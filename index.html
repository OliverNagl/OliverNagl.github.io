Designing Protein Structures from Natural Language

Joseph Lee, …, Pang Wei Koh· arXiv·9 Oct

Trains an all-atom generator on 1.8M structures paired with 50M+ residue-aligned captions, so you can write what you want a protein to look like or do and get a structure conditioned directly on that text, like text-to-image but for backbones.

pdf
abstract

A central challenge in de novo protein design is generating proteins with desired structural and functional properties. Existing design models typically require a dedicated input representation for each property, limiting the design objectives they support. We develop Prompteina, the first all-atom structure generation model that supports new and broader design objectives specified in natural language, analogous to text-to-image generation. To train Prompteina, we construct Prompteina-Data, a paired text--protein structure dataset of 1.8M structures and 50M+ captions describing global and residue-specific structural and functional properties. We adapt Proteina-Complexa, a pretrained structure generation model, through text-conditioned pretraining and finetuning that aligns residues with their corresponding text. Prompteina outperforms baselines on 15 of 18 structural properties and generates proteins that more closely match descriptions of their intended functions. In binder design, it leads baselines on at least 13 of 20 targets and improves interface confidence by 14\% over the strongest baseline. With iterative in silico feedback, Prompteina's gain over non-iterative generation is 57\% larger than the next-best baseline on the hardest targets. Finally, we experimentally validate Prompteina designs against GM2A, a target with no reported de novo binders. We identify nine binders, with the strongest at an apparent $K_{\mathrm{D}}$ of 107 nM. These results establish natural language as an effective and powerful conditioning interface for protein structure generation.

De novo protein design in an expanded chemical space

Li, Y., …, Liu, T.· bioRxiv·9 Oct

Loosens the pair-representation conditioning in a pretrained all-atom structure model while keeping chemical anchors fixed, so noncanonical residues and PTMs become native building blocks the generator can place and denoise around, not post-hoc substitutions.

doipdf
abstract

Generative protein design has made sequence and structure programmable, yet its chemical alphabet remains largely confined to the canonical twenty. Here, we establish CheMoDesign, an experimentally validated generative framework for designing proteins de novo around user-specified chemistry. CheMoDesign weakens pair-representation conditioning in a pretrained all-atom structure-prediction model while preserving chemical anchors, then refines generated backbones through sequence-conditioned denoising. By treating post-translational modifications, synthetic residues, and chemical modifications as user-defined building blocks, the framework generated proteins with chemistry-dependent recognition and reactivity in single-round experimental campaigns. Sulfotyrosine and nitrotyrosine enabled modification-dependent recognition of thrombin and HER2, respectively. Genetically encoded noncanonical residues enabled bioorthogonal affinity switching and light- or proximity-triggered covalent target capture. Post-expression chemical modifications yielded a light-activated covalent PD-L1 binder and an isoform-selective CA IX inhibitor (KD = 45 nM) designed to engage catalytic Zn2+;. CheMoDesign makes chemical mechanism a programmable dimension of de novo protein design.

Design of Orthogonal Far-Red, Orange and Green Fluorophore-binding Proteins for Multiplex Imaging

Tran, L., …, Baker, D.· bioRxiv·9 Oct

Splits a de novo dye-binding protein into two halves that only reconstitute fluorescence when both halves are present at the same subcellular location, turning a designed binder into a proximity sensor, and separately gives the binder a covalent nucleophilic-aromatic-substitution active site to lock onto its dye.

doipdf
abstract

Fluorescent proteins have transformed biological imaging, yet their limited photostability and brightness restrict their applications. We used deep learning-based de novo protein design methods to design binders to three bright, stable and cell-permeable dyes spanning the imaging spectrum: JF657 (far red), JF596 (orange-red) and JF494 (green). We obtain highly specific dye-binding proteins with low nanomolar affinities for the intended target; a crystal structure of one binder confirms close resemblance to the design model. Simultaneous labeling of mammalian cells expressing three dye-specific binders at different subcellular compartments demonstrates the utility in multiplex imaging. We further expand the functionality of the binder by incorporating an active site that carries out nucleophilic aromatic substitution to form a covalent linkage with the dye, and develop split versions which reconstitute fluorescence at subcellular locations where both halves are present towards monitoring in-cell protein interactions and chemically induced dimerization. Our designed high affinity and specificity dye binders open up new opportunities for multiplexed biological imaging.

Filling the Missing Puzzle: Integrating Interaction and Evolutionary Information to Enhance Protein Fitness Prediction in Inverse Folding

Wang, Z., …, Li, W.· bioRxiv·11 Oct

Builds inverse folding around a heterogeneous residue-ligand graph fused with ESM-2 embeddings, so sequence design gets scored on whether it preserves the actual physical contacts to a bound ligand rather than backbone geometry and evolutionary likelihood alone.

doipdfcode
abstract

Protein inverse folding, which infers compatible amino acid sequences from a given backbone structure, is central to rational protein design. Current methods rely largely on sequence recovery rate as the evaluation metric, emphasizing structural compatibility but seldom addressing function. Yet functional activity depends on the proper network of non-covalent interactions-protein-ligand contacts being a prime example. We argue that function-oriented protein design is governed by three complementary constraints: structural, interaction, and evolutionary, none of which are handled in a unified manner by existing pretrained models. To bridge this gap, we introduce zPolaron, a graph neural network built on protein-ligand complexes for functional protein design. zPolaron represents residues and ligand atoms as a heterogeneous graph and jointly encodes structural, interaction, and evolutionary information by fusing physical-interaction-enhanced graph representations with ESM-2 embeddings. Across multiple fitness prediction benchmarks, zPolaron outperforms all baselines, with gains evident across structural, interaction, and evolutionary dimensions. Wet-lab experiments further confirm its utility in functional enzyme screening and design: the optimized CalA6-D205A-S160G mutant achieves a 17.84-fold improvement in activity, while the CalB-I121M mutant yields a 1.91-fold increase. Together, these results show that integrating interaction and evolutionary information fills the missing puzzle of inverse folding, enhancing protein fitness prediction beyond structural compatibility. zPolaron is available at https://github.com/zelixirSH/zPolaron.

Chain of Structures: Connecting Reasoning and Generation for Protein Design

Cai, Q., …, Zhang, Z.· bioRxiv·11 Oct

Alternates a language-reasoning model and a structure-generation model round by round, catalytic core then active site then global backbone then full sequence, so each reasoning step directly conditions the next generation step and the resulting geometry feeds back into the next reasoning decision.

doipdf
abstract

Scientific design requires reasoning in scientific spaces that represent the objects being designed. Building on Scinetics' Chain of Science Tokens, we present chain-of-structures (COS) as a concrete protein-design case study that connects reasoning and generation through explicit structural states. In this technical report, we apply COS to enzyme design, where catalytic function requires precise local geometry within a compatible global fold, making it a demanding test of reasoning from chemical requirements to protein structure. Starting from a reaction specification and target length, COS couples a language reasoning expert with an all-atom generation expert across four rounds: catalytic core, active site, global backbone, and complete sequence and structure. Each reasoning state directly conditions generation, and the resulting structure becomes context for the next decision. Discrete residue constraints and continuous latents connect the two experts, enabling structural supervision of the reasoning interface. On the evaluated benchmark, COS leads the compared baselines on five of six metrics, achieving 57.0% EC3 agreement and a TM-score of 0.729.

kitAb: an automated framework for assay-specific antibody developability modeling and large-scale landscape analysis

Trofimova, E., …, Ramensky, V.· bioRxiv·11 Oct

Instead of one fixed developability score, fits a separate regression per assay from 59 interpretable descriptors, because which physicochemical property actually predicts an assay's readout turns out to depend entirely on which assay you're predicting.

doipdfcode
abstract

Antibody developability is determined by multiple physicochemical properties, and the relevance of individual properties varies among experimental assays. Existing computational approaches, however, often provide fixed assessments or rely on computationally intensive descriptors. We present kitAb, an open-source framework for scalable physicochemical characterization of antibody Fv regions and assay-specific developability modeling. kitAb calculates 59 interpretable sequence- and structure-based descriptors and integrates them with automated feature selection and regression. We evaluated kitAb across ten public datasets comprising 46 experimental endpoints, using sequence-aware or predefined splits where feasible. Predictive performance was strongly assay-dependent. In nested evaluations with sequence-aware or predefined data splits, models using kitAb descriptors achieved predictive performance comparable on average to those using PROPERMAB descriptors, while outperforming models based on TAP or sequence-only features. In a single-structure CPU timing benchmark, kitAb descriptor calculation was approximately 11-fold faster than PROPERMAB. Predictive performance was similar across the three structure-generation backends, and broader AutoML searches did not substantially improve mean performance. We further applied the same descriptor representation to approximately 3.4 million paired natural antibodies and 706 Phase II or later therapeutic antibodies. Repertoire-scale analysis revealed systematic differences in surface charge, hydrophobic and aromatic organization, CDR length and germline-associated physicochemical profiles. The kitAb code and analysis workflows are openly available at https://github.com/katyachemistry/kitAb/.

AutoHMD: Scalable and Accurate Validation of Antibody-Antigen Complexes by Heated Molecular Dynamics.

Souza JS, …, Silva JHM· Bioinformatics (Oxford, England)·9 Oct

Reheats antibody-antigen docking poses and watches which ones hold their interface together under thermal stress, since near-native complexes stay stable while wrong poses fall apart, succeeding where static scoring functions can't tell the two apart.

doicode
abstract

Antibody-antigen (Ab-Ag) interactions are central to immune recognition and widely exploited in therapeutic design, making accurate structural characterization of these complexes a central challenge in the field. While docking and artificial intelligence-based approaches can generate multiple candidate binding modes, reliably identifying near-native conformations represent a key bottleneck. Existing methods depend largely on static scoring functions, which fail to capture the dynamic nature of Ab-Ag interfaces and, as a result, offer limited power to discriminate between competing poses. Here, we evaluate heated molecular dynamics (HMD) as a strategy to discriminate binding pose quality based on structural stability under thermal perturbation, implemented through the automated workflow AutoHMD. Across a diverse set of Ab-Ag complexes, HMD enabled clear separation between high-quality and incorrect poses. During simulations, near-native complexes maintained a stable interface, whereas incorrect ones showed progressive divergence, particularly at elevated temperatures. Interaction analysis revealed that high-quality poses maintain more stable and persistent interfacial contacts throughout the simulations. In contrast, incorrect poses display an unstable interaction network. Additional simulation replicates increased classification consistency, while a mean iRMSD cutoff of approximately 2.5 Å achieved 90% accuracy in cross-validation and retained robust performance on an independent external set. Its transfer to Boltz-2-generated complexes further supported the broader applicability of HMD to AI-predicted binding poses. Overall, AutoHMD provides a reproducible, scalable, and high-throughput framework for validation of predicted antibody-antigen complexes, with potential applications in structural bioinformatics and biopharmaceutical design. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online. The AutoHMD workflow scripts are available on GitHub at https://github.com/SFBBGroup/AutoHMD. The version used in this study is permanently archived in Zenodo at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22757672. All simulation and structural data supporting this study are publicly available in Zenodo at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20722229; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22750139; https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20707612.

Feathers, J. R., …, Zhong, E.· bioRxiv·10 Oct

Builds three separate benchmark datasets that each isolate one normally-entangled failure mode of cryo-EM heterogeneity analysis, compositional mixtures, continuous conformational flexibility, and pose-conformation confound, so algorithms can finally be scored against ground truth on each one independently.

doipdf
abstract

The ability of cryo-electron microscopy (cryo-EM) to interrogate the atomic structure and motion of biomolecules has motivated the development of a wide range of algorithms for heterogeneity analysis. However, evaluating and comparing these methods remains challenging because ground-truth structures are generally unknown for experimental samples. Here, we introduce the 2026 Community-Wide Assessment of Heterogeneous Reconstruction Algorithms (CAHRA), a community-wide challenge centered on three benchmark datasets that probe distinct tasks in heterogeneity analysis. These include (1) compositional heterogeneity arising from mixtures of distinct protein complexes, (2) continuous conformational variability and atomic modeling, and (3) entanglement between molecular conformation and particle pose. We describe the design, construction, and validation of these datasets, as well as their use in the recently completed CAHRA Challenge.

Masked Diffusion Language Agents for Tool-Integrated Chemical Reasoning

Mengdi Liu, …, Shiguang Shan· arXiv·9 Oct

Instead of generating a reasoning-plus-tool-call trajectory left to right, denoises the whole interleaved trajectory at once, so each tool-use decision is made with the full context of what comes before and after it rather than only what came before.

pdfcode
abstract

Designing tool-integrated reasoning agent systems for complex chemical tasks remains a fundamental challenge. Although recent chemical agents have demonstrated promising performance through the integration of external tools, they are still built on the autoregressive paradigm, which is inherently constrained by causal attention and left-to-right generation. As a result, they often struggle with bidirectional molecular understanding, global coordinated tool planning and multi-source evidence integration. To address this challenge, we introduce ChemDiffAgent, the first masked diffusion language agent for tool-integrated chemical reasoning. It reformulates chemical reasoning as iterative denoising over interleaved reasoning and tool-use trajectories, enabling tool-use decisions to be jointly determined under full-trajectory context and thereby better aligning the agent's decision boundary with its knowledge boundary. We further analyze how this paradigm benefits agentic reasoning through harder infilling subproblems during training and flexible arbitrary-order decoding during inference. Then we develop a two-stage post-training framework, comprising agentic supervised fine-tuning and variance-reduced preference optimization, to enhance tool-use and reasoning capabilities. We also construct a comprehensive benchmark covering six chemistry tasks across both single-turn and multi-turn tool-use scenarios. Under matched budgets and evaluation, ChemDiffAgent consistently outperforms autoregressive agents, achieving an overall improvement of approximately 20%. Notably, our 8B model achieves performance comparable to, in some cases surpassing, significantly larger frontier models, such as GPT 5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7. Code is available at https://github.com/bilemond/ChemDiffAgent.

De Novo Design of Flavoproteins with Controlled Excited State Dynamics

Anderson, R., …, Mulholland, A. J.· bioRxiv·10 Oct

Folds QM/MM MD and time-dependent DFT excited-state calculations directly into the design loop, not just post-hoc characterization, so the same thermostable scaffold with and without a heme-binding site lets them dial flavin redox and excited-state behavior while X-ray confirms near-angstrom design accuracy.

doipdf
abstract

Designing photochemical properties in proteins is a frontier challenge in biomolecular design, which, when realised, will open new avenues in catalysis and biomolecular electronics. Here we demonstrate an integrated computational design framework, supported by ultrafast spectroscopy, combining protein design, quantum mechanical/molecular mechanics molecular dynamics (QM/MM MD) and electronic structure calculations, to control flavin excited states in de novo proteins. Iterative design yields FC-4D2, a flavocytochrome with covalently bound flavin and non-covalently bound heme B and Fl-4D2, where the heme-binding site is removed. Both proteins are highly thermostable, and access all physiologically relevant flavin redox and protonation states, while high-resolution X-ray crystallography confirms the near-[A]ngstrom-level accuracy in design. Incorporating QM/MM MD and linear-response time-dependent density functional theory (LR-TDDFT) into the design workflow enables engineering of a flavin-tryptophan charge-transfer variant, Fl-4D2CT, that modifies the flavin absorption spectrum, substantially alters its redox properties and suppresses fluorescence. Ultrafast studies reveal how protein design reshapes the excited state relaxation timescale and mechanism. Our study establishes a modular strategy for programming flavin photochemistry in designed proteins.

How Fluctuations Control Chiral Symmetry Breaking in Reacting Mixtures of Chiral Molecules

Leonardo Silva-Dias, Christoph A. Weber· arXiv·9 Oct

This models chiral symmetry breaking in small reacting populations as a stochastic, fluctuation-driven selection problem rather than a thermodynamic one, deriving how finite-population noise decides which of several equivalent states a system commits to and how long that commitment takes.

Symmetry-native tied-ASU diffusion and quasi-equivalence both assume a subunit's commitment to one of several degenerate symmetric assembly outcomes is governed by free energy, but this paper's analytical treatment of finite-population fluctuations deciding between mirror-symmetric outcomes suggests assembly-slot or conformer choice in quasi-equivalent capsids may instead be a kinetic, fluctuation-dominated branching process. The same bias-dependent selection-time formalism could motivate building design-quality oracles around stochastic commitment probabilities rather than static thermodynamic scores.

pdfconfidence medium
abstract

The pronounced chiral asymmetry of biomolecules is a defining feature of living systems, yet the mechanisms by which such asymmetry emerged remain unresolved. Chiral symmetry breaking may have occurred under prebiotic conditions in systems with sufficiently small molecular populations for intrinsic fluctuations to play an important role. Here, we investigate how finite-population fluctuations affect chiral-state selection, the characteristic time for symmetry breaking, and stochastic switching between chiral states in a system driven by autocatalysis and mutual antagonism. We determine how these dynamical processes scale with particle number and derive an analytical expression for the probability of selecting either chiral state as a function of the initial enantiomeric bias. We further calculate the mean selection time for different initial biases and show that, for weak mutual antagonism, fluctuations can induce transitions between opposite chiral states. These results characterize how finite-population fluctuations control both the emergence and stability of molecular chirality in autocatalytic chemical systems.
