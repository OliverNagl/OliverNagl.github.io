MYC targeted de novo binders impair tumor growth

McNamara-Bordewick, N. K., …, Baker, D.· bioRxiv·16 Oct

Designed binders target conserved intrinsically disordered epitopes on MYC itself, depleting MYC-dependent tumor cells across five cancer models where small molecules have never worked.

doipdf
abstract

MYC is one of the most frequently dysregulated oncogenic drivers across many cancers, yet direct therapeutic targeting has remained difficult because its function is mediated by disordered regions and transient protein-protein interactions. Here we design proteins that bind epitopes within regions of MYC whose sequence is conserved between human and mouse, including intrinsically disordered motifs required for oncogenic activity. In vitro, the designs bind MYC with nanomolar affinity and in cells they engage MYC, disrupt interactions with MAX, and remodel MYC-associated protein complexes. Expression of the binders decreases cell proliferation across diverse MYC-dependent hematopoietic, liver, pancreatic, and patient-derived tumor models, and in an orthotopic pancreatic cancer model, binder-expressing tumor cells were selectively depleted. This work establishes de novo protein design as a generalizable strategy to therapeutically target intrinsically disordered transcription factors.

Complex enzyme active site scaffolding by iteratively detuned catalytic guidance

Qian, T., …, Baker, D.· bioRxiv·16 Oct

Fades classifier-free guidance on RF3 over repeated design cycles so a sequence settles into satisfying several catalytic-geometry constraints at once, instead of fighting them simultaneously.

doipdf
abstract

De novo enzyme design requires sequences whose structures reproduce prescribed catalytic geometries. This remains challenging for active sites that couple multiple catalytic residues, cofactors and substrates. Here we introduce the Catalytic Guidance Engine (CaGE), which optimizes designed sequences to satisfy complex sets of functional constraints by alternating constraint guided structure prediction trajectories with sequence design, gradually fading the strength of the constraint information over cycles to nudge the designed sequence towards satisfying all the constraints. We train RF3 to accept atom-pair distances and other design conditions, and use classifier-free guidance to fade their influence over multiple design cycles. We also develop training-free motif guidance protocols which operate at inference time by directly adjusting selected atoms towards the design target geometry during diffusion sampling. CaGE improves catalytic-motif geometry across a diverse enzyme benchmark and yields sequences that recover complex target active sites in independent AF3 predictions. We experimentally demonstrate the power of CaGE by designing active enzymes with complex catalytic sites: a nonheme-iron design positions four active-site residues around an iron centre and a transition-state ligand, and a PLP-dependent design positions four catalytic residues around a PLP cofactor with multiple functional groups. Active site substitutions and a 2.7[A] crystal structure support the designed mechanisms and structures. Thus, CaGE provides a route to functional de novo enzymes with complex active sites.

Generative steering to adapt proteins beyond native biological contexts

Zhang, X., …, Gao, X. J.· bioRxiv·18 Oct

A test-time annealing sampler converts secreted human renin into an active cytosolic protease and strips HLA-binder epitopes from antibody fragments, without retraining the generator.

doipdf
abstract

Proteins evolved for native biological contexts often fail when deployed in new cellular environments or therapeutic settings. We present ProVADA, a test-time framework that steers a pretrained protein generator toward externally defined properties while preserving functional constraints. Its Mixture-Adaptation Directed Annealing sampler combines coordinated mutations, population selection, and annealing to navigate rugged objective landscapes. Experimentally, ProVADA converted secreted human renin into an active, drug-inhibitable cytosolic protease and extensively deimmunized two murine single-chain antibody fragments to reduce HLA visibility while retaining antigen recognition. ESCAPE-seq measurements confirmed substantial depletion of HLA-I binder peptides across alleles. Deimmunized FMC63 variants retained CAR-T-cell killing and showed fewer binders than a tested humanized comparator. Together, these results demonstrate that broad sequence redesign can adapt functional proteins to non-native biological contexts.

Structural analysis of de novo-designed binders targeting the closed state of HSP90.

Srivastava D, …, Artemyev NO· Structure (London, England : 1993)·16 Oct

Cryo-EM confirms BindCraft's predicted structures atom-for-atom, and the two designed binders selectively lock HSP90's closed conformation, a state no small-molecule inhibitor has ever reached.

doi
abstract

Heat shock protein 90 (HSP90) assists the folding and maturation of numerous signaling proteins and is an important therapeutic target. Most HSP90 inhibitors target the ATP-binding pocket and disrupt ATP-induced closure, whereas inhibitors targeting the closed state have not been developed. Here, we report de novo-designed protein binders that selectively recognize closed HSP90. Two binders, H2 and H4, have similar folds and affinities for HSP90 but distinct binding kinetics. Cryo-EM structures of HSP90 complexes with H2 and H4 revealed high accuracy of the BindCraft predictions. H2 and H4 compete with p23 at one, but not both, symmetrical p23-binding sites. In HEK293T cells, H2 induced a reduced heat shock response compared with the ATP-site inhibitor 17-DMAG while reducing levels of CDK4, providing functional evidence for HSP90 inhibition. These binders provide tools for probing HSP90 function and potential leads for developing closed-state HSP90 inhibitors.

Rigidity-Aware Geometric Pretraining for Protein Design and Conformational Ensembles

Ni, Z., …, Liu, S.· bioRxiv·18 Oct

Pretrains on 432K AlphaFold-DB structures under simulated rigid-body perturbations before generative finetuning, front-loading geometric understanding instead of learning it incidentally from design labels.

doipdfcode
abstract

Generative models have recently advanced de novo protein design by learning the statistical regularities of natural structures. However, current approaches face three key limitations: (1) Existing methods cannot jointly learn protein geometry and design tasks, where pretraining can be a solution; (2) Current pretraining methods mostly rely on local, non-rigid atomic representations for property prediction downstream tasks, limiting global geometric understanding for protein generation tasks; and (3) Existing approaches have yet to effectively model the rich dynamic and conformational information of protein structures. To overcome these issues, we introduce RigidSSL (Rigidity-Aware Self-Supervised Learning}, a geometric pretraining framework that front-loads geometry learning prior to generative finetuning. Phase I (RigidSSL-Perturb) learns geometric priors from 432K structures from the AlphaFold Protein Structure Database with simulated perturbations. Phase II (RigidSSL-MD) refines these representations on 1.3K molecular dynamics trajectories to capture physically realistic transitions. Underpinning both phases is a bi-directional, rigidity-aware flow matching objective that jointly optimizes translational and rotational dynamics to maximize mutual information between conformations. Empirically, RigidSSL variants improve designability by up to 43% while enhancing novelty and diversity in unconditional generation. Furthermore, RigidSSL-Perturb improves the success rate by 5.8% in zero-shot motif scaffolding and RigidSSL-MD captures more biophysically realistic conformational ensembles in G protein-coupled receptor modeling. The code is available at: https://github.com/ZhanghanNi/RigidSSL.git.

De novo design of metalloenzymes using Metal Mer

Shan, C., …, Lu, L.· bioRxiv·16 Oct

Metal Mer maps backbone geometry directly onto statistically preferred metal-coordination modes, then builds the catalytic core around the product, reaching >99% ee on 13 substrates with no directed evolution.

doipdf
abstract

De novo enzyme design provides a route to create biocatalysts for target reactions, yet achieving high activity and selectivity directly from computation remains challenging. How best to design catalytic sites remains an open question, and enzyme design-metalloenzyme design for new-to-nature transformations in particular-is still limited in success rate, in substrate scope, and in the range of reactions accessible. Here we present a pipeline that produces efficient metalloenzymes directly from computation. It uses Metal Mer, a structural unit mapping backbone geometry to statistically preferred metal coordination modes, to build metal-binding sites with high confidence, and then constructs the catalytic core around the reaction product with geometric restrictions. Using the pipeline, we designed zinc-dependent hydride-transfer enzymes, obtaining a highly selective variant that exhibits enantiomeric excess (ee) >99% on 13 substrates without directed evolution. For steroid substrates bearing multiple carbonyl groups, redesigned variants achieved high regio- and diastereo- selectivity greater than 19:1. Applying this framework to iron-catalyzed alkene difunctionalization, designed variants were active out of the box (10% yield, 70:30 enantiomeric ratio (e.r.)), exceeding a previously reported natural functional analogs (<5% yield, <60:40 e.r.); a single active-site residue mutation further raised selectivity to 96:4 e.r. for several substrates, on par with extensively evolved natural enzymes. The crystal structure of an active design matches the computational model with a backbone RMSD of 0.4 Angstrom, supporting the accuracy of the approach. These results establish our design framework as a general strategy for the de novo design of metalloenzyme series with diverse coordination geometries and coordinating residues, enabling efficient and selective catalysis of challenging chemical transformations.

Seq2Pocket: Augmenting protein language models for spatially consistent binding site prediction.

Škrhák V, …, Hoksza D· Bioinformatics (Oxford, England)·16 Oct

Smooths residue-level pLM binding-site predictions with embedding-aware post-processing so the output pocket is one contiguous patch instead of scattered false fragments.

doicode
abstract

Protein-ligand binding site prediction (LBS) is important for many areas of structural biology and molecular modeling, where, as in other tasks, protein language models (pLMs) have shown great promise. In their application to LBS, the pLM classifies each amino acid as binding or not, but translating these predictions into three-dimensional binding pockets remains challenging; in particular, residue-centric predictions tend to produce spatially fragmented pockets. We present Seq2Pocket, a methodology for pocket-level LBS prediction that combines pLM finetuning, data enhancement, and structure-aware post-processing. First, we introduce sc-PDBenhanced, an extended training dataset that augments an existing sc-PDB dataset with additional small-molecule and ion-binding sites, improving coverage of non-obvious and small pockets. Second, we employ an embedding-supported smoothing classifier to refine residue-level predictions. Third, we define the Pocket Fragmentation Index and use it to select a clustering approach that preserves a consistent mapping between predictions and ground-truth pockets. We evaluate Seq2Pocket on two tasks: general binding site prediction using the LIGYSIS benchmark and cryptic binding site prediction using CryptoBench benchmark. Across both benchmarks, Seq2Pocket achieves state-of-the-art DCCtop-N and DCCtop-(N+2) performance. In particular, for general binding site prediction on the LIGYSIS benchmark, it improves distance-center-to-center recall by up to 13%, outperforming existing predictors. This improvement originates from training data quality rather than model architecture, as demonstrated by an ablation study. We believe that these findings contribute to more reliable evaluation practices in ligand binding site prediction, highlight the importance of training data curation, and provide pocket-level prediction tools that are better suited for downstream applications such as drug discovery. The Seq2Pocket web interface is available at https://seq2pocket.projekty.ms.mff.cuni.cz, running the ESM-2 650M variant. The sc-PDBenhanced is published using Zenodo platform at https://zenodo.org/records/18271517 [Škrhák and Hoksza 2026]. The models are published at HuggingFace: https://huggingface.co/skrhakv/seq2pocket. Supplementary data are available at Bioinformatics online.

GOLD: Geometric Optimized Latent Diffusion for Structure-Aware RNA Inverse Folding

Qi Si, …, Yuan Cheng· arXiv·16 Oct

Projects discrete RNA sequence and continuous side-chain angles into one shared latent manifold, so backbone and sequence co-diffuse instead of being generated in separate, incompatible spaces.

pdfcode
abstract

Designing RNA sequences that fold into specific 3D structures is a fundamental challenge in therapeutics. Current RNA inverse folding methods typically condition on 3D backbone coordinates but the generation is limited to 1D discrete sequences. This ignores the continuous side-chain orientations, which are essential for overall structural stability. To address this issue, we explicitly introduce side-chain geometry into the RNA generative process. Unlike co-design frameworks that diffuse across separate raw spaces, we propose Geometric Optimized Latent Diffusion (\textbf{GOLD}), which project sequence and structure into a unified latent manifold. GOLD employs a novel GeoVAE to elegantly integrate discrete RNA sequences with continuous side-chain angles. By mapping these modalities into a single, smooth latent manifold, GOLD naturally facilitates a synergistic co-diffusion process. To enhance structural validity, we further introduce a training-free, gradient-guided sampling mechanism. By decoding the generated sequence probabilities and side-chain geometries into structures during the sampling process, we compute analytical gradients from physical constraints. This dynamic feedback actively guides the generated sequence to a physically stable state. Extensive experiments validate the superiority of GOLD, achieving state-of-the-art performance across sequence recovery, secondary structure validity, and 3D foldability metrics. Our code and data are publicly available at https://github.com/darkflash03/GOLD.

Relation-Aware Graph-Hypergraph Networks for Antibody Sequence-Structure Co-Design

Wei, J., …, GUO, J.· bioRxiv·18 Oct

Models the antibody-antigen interface as a hypergraph instead of pairwise edges, so cooperative multi-residue effects in CDR design finally have an architecture built for them.

doipdf
abstract

Motivation: The design of antibody complementarity-determining regions (CDRs) is pivotal for therapeutic development, yet remains challenged by the complexity of the antibody-antigen interface. Existing methods, which largely rely on pairwise relationships, struggle to capture intrinsic multi-body interactions and global structural constraints. Results: We propose the Relation-Aware Graph-Hypergraph Network (RA-GHN), a framework that unifies graph-based pairwise modeling with biophysical hypergraphs to capture high-order cooperative effects. Its heterogeneous hypergraph uses epitope-paratope interaction patches, spatial structural clusters, and high-degree hub structures. A dual-pathway architecture integrates a Relation-Aware Equivariant Graph Network with an Equivariant Hypergraph Encoder while preserving SE(3)-equivariance. We also introduce the Structure-Sequence Decoupling Index, which balances structural reliability with sequence novelty. Experiments show that RA-GHN outperforms state-of-the-art baselines across standard benchmarks and affinity optimization tasks.

LigRNA: High-accuracy prediction of RNA-ligand binding sites via dynamic chemical complementarity and language model encoding

Wang, X., …, Xiong, P.· bioRxiv·17 Oct

Encodes ligands as attributed molecular graphs rather than fixed fingerprints, then cross-attends them against structure-aware RNA embeddings to predict exact binding residues instead of just binding or not.

doipdfcode
abstract

RNA-binding small molecules represent a validated therapeutic modality, yet pinpointing their exact RNA binding sites remains critical for mechanistic understanding and drug optimization. Experimental methods, while precise, are costly and low-throughput, necessitating robust computational alternatives. Approaches based on static feature concatenation may overlook input-dependent relationships between RNA residues and ligand functional groups. Here we present LigRNA, a graph neural network framework that predicts RNA-ligand binding sites through joint cross-modal encoding of both molecules. LigRNA integrates structure-guided language model embeddings with physicochemical features-solvent-accessible surface area and secondary structure types, to construct comprehensive RNA representations. Ligands are encoded as attributed molecular graphs via graph attention networks, preserving atom-level chemical topology rather than compressing molecules into fixed fingerprints. A cross-attention mechanism then enables dynamic, ligand-dependent modulation of RNA nucleotide attention, capturing context-specific interaction patterns. Benchmarked on TE9 and TE18 datasets, LigRNA achieves F1 scores of 0.73 and 0.58, improving on GATRsite by 7.4% and 18.4%, respectively. Ablation studies identify the RNA residual encoder as the most consistent contributor across the two benchmarks. The source code is freely available at https://github.com/WillowWang0216/LigRNA.

Kinetic-driven microphase separation and vitrification of crowded hydrogels with associative bonds.

Shi D, …, Gong JP· Science advances·16 Oct

A crowded associative-bond hydrogel resolves into either homogeneous vitrification or confined microphase separation depending purely on the competing timescales of elastic relaxation versus diffusive demixing along the approach trajectory, not on which state is the free-energy minimum.

This is a concrete kinetic-trap mechanism for the assembly-oracle problem: a subunit thermodynamically capable of closing into a shell can still arrest into an off-pathway aggregate if local bond-formation outruns global diffusive reorganization. An oracle trained only on structural or thermodynamic features of the target state, with no path-rate term, would systematically miss exactly this failure mode.

doiconfidence medium
abstract

The thermal response of stimuli-responsive hydrogels is typically viewed through thermodynamic equilibrium. However, in "crowded" networks with dense associative bonds, elasticity and diffusion kinetics dictate distinct nonequilibrium fates. We investigate thermal transitions of a PAAc-CaAc2 (polyacrylic acid complexed with calcium ions) hydrogel along two trajectories: a quasi-static isobaric pathway (path A) and a rapid quasi-isochoric pathway (path B). Along path A, the gel contracts homogeneously until reaching a hardening temperature via global vitrification (Thard). In contrast, path B triggers confined microphase separation into polymer-dense and dilute phases at a critical temperature (Tdem), followed by a hardening transition via localized vitrification ([Formula: see text]). We demonstrate that Tdem is governed by a mechanical-thermodynamic competition: The internal pressure (Pc) to trigger demixing scales linearly with the permanent-network shear modulus (Gx), maintaining a ratio of Pc/Gx ≈ 100. We identify Thard and [Formula: see text] as distinct hardening points, with elastically constrained local water content governing [Formula: see text]. This framework provides a strategy for programming nonequilibrium fates and "lockable" properties of responsive soft matter.
