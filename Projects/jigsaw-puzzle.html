Double Solution Jigsaw Puzzles

Generating jigsaw puzzles that have exactly two non-trivial solutions

pySAT Python Group Theory SAT

Overview

A personal project to generate jigsaw puzzles that can be assembled in exactly two non-trivial ways. It was inspired by Matt Parker's video "How can a jigsaw have two distinct solutions" and by diffusion illusions (Burgert et al.). Where Parker's brute-force search tops out around 6×6, encoding the problem as a SAT instance scales it to 15×15 pieces.

How it works

The generator turns the puzzle into a Boolean satisfiability (SAT) problem and solves it:

1

Generate & encode

Start from a random valid puzzle and encode each piece's edge-connection types and grid positions as SAT variables and constraints.

2

Rule out trivial solutions

Add constraints that disallow global rotations and simple row/column swaps, so the second solution is genuinely different.

3

Solve & verify

Use the pySAT solver to find the second solution and confirm no third one exists — exactly two arrangements. A 15×15 puzzle solves overnight on a laptop.
